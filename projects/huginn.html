Why Huginn

Conventional drones are loud and obvious. A quadcopter overhead tells everyone they're being watched, and its batteries are flat in under half an hour. Huginn takes a different approach, borrowed from the bird it's named after. In Norse myth, Huginn is one of Odin's two ravens, sent out each day to see the world and report back.

Its raven planform and slotted wingtip "feathers" let it glide efficiently at low speed and blend into the sky. When it doesn't need to fly, it doesn't: Huginn is designed to land on an approved perch, fold its propeller and keep watch from a fixed vantage point, using a fraction of the energy of hovering. A solar-film wing tops up the battery in daylight.

The result is a quiet, persistent set of eyes for event security, perimeter overwatch and site awareness, operated inside the rules (FAA Part 107, Remote ID, visual line of sight).

Capabilities

  • Blends in

    A raven silhouette and natural flight style, so it reads as a bird, not a drone, at working altitudes.

  • Perch and stare

    It lands on an approved high point, folds its prop and watches. Designed for ≈ 10.9 h of AI watch on battery; sun on the perch may extend it (design estimate).

  • Solar-assisted glide

    An efficient gliding wing with integrated solar film stretches flight time on sunny days.

  • On-board AI vision

    Stereo depth camera with on-device AI for perch-site assessment and object detection. No cloud required.

  • Safety first

    A pilot's manual override is always available, with automatic return-home failsafes, Remote ID broadcast and visual-line-of-sight operation.

  • Built for the field

    A modular airframe that can be printed and repaired in the field, with a foam wing and replaceable parts.
