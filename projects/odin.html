    Overview

    Why ODIN

    Security, emergency-response and remote-site teams often work where the internet is unavailable, unreliable or not allowed. ODIN is the hardware that goes with them: a Raspberry Pi 5 node that powers on, broadcasts its own ODINet5G Wi-Fi hotspot and serves everything the team needs from the box. Nothing leaves it.

    ODIN runs Overwatch Tactical, the offline edition of our Overwatch workforce software, alongside its own local database, maps, reference library, voice, file sharing and radio tools. In Norse myth, Odin sends out his ravens to see the world and report back; this node is where field information comes home.

    What it runs

    Services on the node

    • Overwatch Tactical, offline

      Our operations software runs locally: incidents, patrols, team chat and messaging over the mesh.

    • Operations map

      Live team positions on offline map tiles, with TAK-style unit symbols, routes and markup, built to be simple on a phone.

    • BIFROST LoRa mesh

      A native mesh for text and positions beyond Wi-Fi, with Meshtastic and MeshCore interop.

    • SDR scanner + transcription

      A software-defined radio scans local channels and transcribes traffic on the device.

    • Offline library

      A Kiwix library of field medicine, self-reliance, radio and other reference books.

    • Mumble voice

      Low-bandwidth push-to-talk voice over the hotspot.

    • File drop

      Share files to and from the node, with optional password-protected folders.

    • ODINet5G hotspot

      Its own 5 GHz Wi-Fi network. Join it and open any service in a browser.

    • Local PocketBase

      The node keeps its own database, so data stays on the box.

    Radio

    BIFROST

    Backbone Infrastructure for Field Radio Operations using Spread-spectrum Transceivers. BIFROST is ODIN's radio link: a native LoRa mesh designed around security, performance and throughput, with interop so stock Meshtastic and MeshCore radios can join the picture.

    Planned next: one portable firmware for many boards (ESP32, nRF52 and RP2040 radios) and a BIFROST flasher served straight from the node, so radios can be set up in the field with no internet.
